Someone called TigerBeetle the Rolls Royce of software engineering and it made me chuckle because it's true -- the TigerBeetle database is great software, but the car metaphor runs deeper than that. Software companies today are built one at a time and cost a minimum of a quarter million just to get your foot in the door, like a Rolls Royce, but I expect we'll see the cost of creating a software company come down to as low as $10,000, with operational expenses at around $2-4,000 a year (compute costs money, even if it's on prem in your home office).

Basically like owning a car. The twist is that having a software company is way more useful than owning a car.

Not having a software company in 2026 is straight up unfair the same way having a car is unfair in the average American town, or the way having guns in Manchuria was unfair in 1287, or cannons in 1453 Constantinople. If you can orchestrate a fleet of compute to do useful things like generate and stream media, run CI, make purchases and sell stuff, schedule reservations, and analyze trends, and do it all from your phone, you will run laps around any human not doing any of that.

The function of a software company is to digitize the physical world, and move through that digitized space.

Software companies turn compute into useful behavior. Computers teach rocks how to think. Software companies teach rocks how to act.

So the folks building personal assistants are thinking too small. If we're competing in a hyper-efficient market, your OpenClaw is going to get lapped by my OpenClaw armed with hundreds of CPU cores, terabytes of RAM, and sophisticated ETL pipelines aggregating sensor data across the world.

If your agent and my agent ever negotiate, your agent will be slaughtered. Don't give them a knife and send them into a gun fight.

This is exactly what keeps me up at night. The difference in capabilities between agents looks less like a Prius vs a Porsche and more like coughing baby vs hydrogen bomb. If we're not careful, the future can easily be ruled by a Jane Street apex predator agent adversely selecting us all as permanent members of the underclass.

That's why I founded my company, Guardian, and created [Verself](https://github.com/guardian-intelligence/verself). It's an open-source software company.

Software companies won't be built by hand from the ground up, they're too useful to leave their creation to a small group who know all the ins and outs. Same reason we had an entire era of this country dedicated to making physical manufacturing as efficient as possible, with cars as the central consumer good.

I expect Software companies will be stamped out like injection molds.

The TigerBeetle database was built with real craftsmanship: deterministic simulation testing, zero dependencies, hand-written, cage-free.
