I wish someone told me having a son would feel like meeting a smaller, kinder, funnier version of myself. Someone who holds my quirks like treasures instead of flaws.

I love how he stops our morning walks to teach me the laughing gulls' names. There was a new one today he didn’t recognize. It’s “Donnie,” just so you know.

And did you know he has a crush? He told me during breakfast that when they're around, his stomach tries to escape through his chest, and he asked me what he should do. It reminded me of when we met -- those first few months -- and falling in love at our first Odesza. So I smiled and told him, “Find out what their favorite game is, then let them watch you play it!” He rolled his eyes at me because he’s sick of hearing the story, but I wonder what you’d say if he asked you.

I love the way he puts back together parts of me that I forgot were even broken. He's been practicing his laugh in the car. “So it can sound just like yours,” he tells me. And I love the way he holds out way more tissues than I need when he makes me cry. The tears help me see that there’s a crack in everything. That’s how his light gets in.

Yesterday, while everyone was visiting, he got really quiet and walked past everyone to tug on my sleeve. I was worried at first, but all he asked was if I could, next year, homeschool him and all his classmates from last year. He wants me to teach them every year until they all graduate from college together. He loudly counted up the grades as he was explaining, but I had to stop him when he got to the twenties.

I asked him why. He said, “They beg me to ask you every day since summer started.” He gave me permission to skip math if that would make it easier on me. I couldn’t argue. “And I wish the house was like this all the time -- full of friends.”

He shows me every day that love and curiosity are two sides of the same thing. I cherish the way his eyes go wide when he flips over seashells and the worms squirm underneath and the dreams he tells me about where he’s a bird searching the sky for clouds that look like you.

Last night before bed, I was making sure he brushed his teeth. Do you remember how it took years to get him to stop spitting out his toothpaste? He called it “spicy ice cream” just one time then decided he wants to try every flavor.

And I didn’t know having a daughter would be like getting to meet the two of us as one tiny human, but braver and wiser. Someone who knocks on my door when I’m crying because I’m sad, and charges through it when I cry because I’ve been hurt.

I mentioned needing something from the market the other day and she jumped up from editing a music video to beg me to let her come. I was hoping she would because I adore the way she finds magic in things I stopped looking at. There was one stall selling jewelry for pennies, and when we passed by she froze, her entire being enchanted by an aquamarine seahorse necklace. She detailed to me at a few hundred words per minute how each of her favorite characters would love or hate the necklace in different ways. She no longer stops herself because she knows now how much I admire the depth of her explanations.

By the time we got home and parked, she was only halfway done. “It’ll be easier if I make a flow chart,” she said, and raced up to her room.

I treasure the scrapbooks piling up from our adventures. A million branches and she loves every one. And I worry about her, too. There's something in the way she trembles standing between her friends and a spider, and the way she cried all morning because the spring flowers were gone. It's like a poet and a warrior made a home in her heart but they’re still learning to share.

Over lunch, I finished the shield from her XIV character. The one she’s been asking me to make for months. When I gave it to her, she screamed and ran around the block with it so fast the dogs nor I could keep up. She suddenly stopped, turned around, pushed the shield into my chest and said, “If we only have one, I want you to have it. To keep you safe.”

I asked what would keep her safe and she furrowed one brow and said, “You, silly!” and did you know that pride and grief can be the same thing?

You should see how tender she is with the parts of me that I used to hide. She tore her favorite cargo shorts the other day climbing the tree where the woodpeckers live. She acted like she's fine, but during dinner she barely ate Mom’s sinigang. So I happened to need her help afterward to clean out the attic, and when we found my old sewing machine that I haven’t used since PAX, her whole face came back to life. “Is this how you made Meteion?” she asked, and then demanded I teach her how to use it. So I did.

I sat with her and watched her sew and get so focused neither of us said a word and all that you could hear was the whir of the machine while the sun sank into the ocean outside. When it felt right, I asked her what had been making her sad, and she didn’t say anything for a long time and then reached into her pocket and put a polaroid into my hand. It had been torn apart, probably by the woodpeckers, but she had affectionately taped it back up. I think you took this one. It’s me and her last year: I was holding her up to the sun, doing a Lion King bit.

She said “You didn’t pick me up once this whole summer", and paused. “Did I grow up too much?” and something about that made me cry but she jumped into my arms and hugged me tight. “I wish I could be a baby again,” she said through tears. I told her that no matter how much she grows up she’ll always be my little baby, and she liked that better and fell asleep on my lap and we stayed like that for I don’t know how long.

No one told me having kids would feel like having a compass in my heart that always points home. That no matter how much we fight and yell and say things we don't mean, that no matter how much our family breaks, our hearts find their way back to each other and we become strong in the broken places.

No one told me any of this, but it's better, I think, because I got to learn it from them.

Anyway, remember to charge the car before we head out this year. If we get stuck outside Leavenworth without AC again, I told them to sing Gentleman in your ear until you promise to get us Cane’s.

💙,
