I wish someone told me having a son would feel like meeting a smaller, kinder, funnier version of myself. Someone who holds my quirks like treasures instead of flaws.

I love how he sometimes stops our morning walks to tell me names of laughing gulls. There was a new one today he didn’t recognize. It’s “Donnie,” just so you know.

I found out during breakfast that he has a crush. He told me that last time they were around, his stomach tried to escape through his chest, and he wanted to know what to do. It reminded me of when we met, those first few months, and falling in love at our first Odesza. So I smiled and told him, “Find out what their favorite game is, then ask them to watch you play it!” He rolled his eyes at me because he’s sick of hearing the story, but I wonder what you’d say if he asked you.

I love hearing him practice his laugh in the car. “So it can sound just like yours,” he tells me. And I love the way he holds out way too many tissues for me when he makes me cry, the way he puts back together parts of me that I forgot were even broken. There’s a crack in everything, by the way. That’s how his light gets in.

Yesterday, while everyone was visiting, he got really quiet and walked past everyone to tug on my sleeve. I was worried at first, but all he asked was if I could, next year, homeschool him and all his classmates from last year. He wants me to teach them every year until they all graduate from college together. He loudly counted up the grades as he was explaining, but I had to stop him when he got to the twenties.

I asked him why. He said, “They begged to ask you, ever since school ended.” He gave me permission to skip math if that would make it easier on me. I couldn’t argue. “And I wish the house was like this all the time -- full of friends.”

He shows me every day that love and curiosity are two sides of the same thing. I cherish the way his eyes go wide when he flips over seashells and the worms squirm underneath and the dreams he tells me about where he’s a bird searching the sky for clouds that look like you.

Last night before bed, I was making sure he brushed his teeth. Do you remember how it took years to get him to stop spitting out his toothpaste? He called it “spicy ice cream” just one time then decided he wants to try every flavor.

And I didn’t know having a daughter would be like getting to meet the two of us as one tiny human, but braver and wiser. Someone who knocks on my door when I’m crying because I’m sad, and barges through it when I cry because I’ve been hurt.

I mentioned needing something from the market and she jumped up from editing a music video to beg me to let her come. I was hoping she would because I adore the way she finds magic in things I stopped looking at. There was one stall selling jewelry for pennies, and when we passed by she froze, her entire being enchanted by an aquamarine seahorse necklace. She detailed to me at a few hundred words per minute how each of her favorite characters would love or hate the necklace in different ways. She no longer stops herself because she knows now how much I admire the depth of her explanations.

By the time we got home and parked, she was only halfway done. “It’ll be easier if I make a flow chart,” she said, and raced up to her room.

I treasure all the scrapbooks from our adventures -- a million branches and she loves every one. And I worry about her, too. It’s like a poet and a warrior live in her heart but they’re still learning to share it. I see them both in the way she trembles standing between her friends and a spider, and when she cried all morning because the spring flowers were gone.

Over lunch, I finished the shield from her XIV character that she’s been asking me to make for months. When I gave it to her, she screamed and ran around the block with it so fast neither the dogs nor I could keep up. When she finally stopped, she turned around, pushed the shield into my chest, and said, “If we only have one, I want you to have it. To keep you safe.”

When I asked her what would keep her safe, she furrowed one brow and said, “You, silly!” and that’s when I found out that pride and grief are the same thing sometimes.

You should see how tender she is with the parts of me that I used to hide. The other day she tore her favorite cargo shorts climbing the tree where the woodpeckers live. She pretended she was fine, but during dinner she barely ate Mom’s sinigang. So I happened to need her help afterward to clean out the attic, and when we found my old sewing machine that I haven’t used since PAX, her whole face came back to life. “Is this how you made Meteion?” she asked, and then demanded I teach her how to use it.

I sat with her and watched her sew, neither of us saying a word, listening to the whir of the machine and the occasional chair creak. I eventually asked her what had been making her sad, and she didn’t say anything for a long time, but when she hit a good stopping point she reached into her pocket and put a polaroid into my hand. It had been torn apart, probably by the woodpeckers, but she had taped it back up. You took this one. It’s me and her last year: I was holding her up to the sun, doing a Lion King bit.

“You didn’t pick me up once this whole summer,” she said, and paused. “Did I grow up too much?” and that made me cry but she jumped into my arms and hugged me tight. “I wish I could be a baby again,” she said through tears. I told her that no matter how much she grows up she’ll always be my baby, and she liked that better and fell asleep on my lap and we stayed like that for I don’t know how long.

No one told me that having kids would be like having a compass in my heart that always points home. That no matter how much we fight and yell and break each other’s hearts, our little family becomes strong in the broken places.

I think it’s better that no one told me any of this, actually, because I got to learn it from them.

Anyway, remember to charge the car before we head out this year. If we get stuck outside Leavenworth without AC again, I’ve told them to sing “Gentleman” in your ear until you promise to get us Cane’s.

💙,
