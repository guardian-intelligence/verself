I wish someone told me that having a son would feel like meeting a smaller, kinder, funnier version of myself. Someone who holds my quirks like treasures instead of flaws.

I love our morning walks and the way he stops us on our tracks to tell me the names of the laughing gulls. There was a new one today he didn’t recognize. It’s “Donnie”, just so you know.

I found out during breakfast that he has a crush. When they're around, his tummy feels like its trying to fly through his heart and he asked me what to do. It reminded me of when we met — those first few months — and falling in love at our first Odesza. So I smiled and told him “you should play their favorite game with them!” He rolled his eyes because he’s sick of hearing me recount our story but I wonder what you’d say, if he asked you.

I love how he practices his laugh in the car. “So it can sound just like yours”, he tells me. And I love the way he holds out too many tissues for me when I tear up at how sweet he is. Something else you should know: there’s a crack in everything. That’s how his light gets in.

Yesterday, while everyone was over, he got really quiet and came over and tugged on my sleeve. I was worried at first but all he asked was if we can home-school him and all his classmates from last year,. He wants me to teach them every year until they all graduate college together. He loudly counted up the grades as he was explaining but I had to stop him when he got to the twenties.

I asked him why. He said “so that my friends always have you around. They missed you all summer”. He gave me permission to skip math if that’d make it easier for me. I couldn’t argue.

He shows me every day that love and curiosity are actually the same thing. I cherish the the way his eyes go wide when he flips over rocks and the worms squirm underneath. Or when he pretends he’s a bird, flying above the clouds to find one that looks like you.

Last night before bed, I was making sure he brushed his teeth. Do you remember how it took years to get him to stop spitting out his toothpaste? He calls it “spicy ice cream” one time and then decided he loves it.

And I didn’t know having a daughter would be like getting to meet the two of us as one tiny human, but braver and wiser. Someone who knocks on my door when I’m crying because I’m sad, and barges through it when I cry because I’ve been hurt.

I mentioned needing something from the market and she jumped up from editing a music video to beg me to come. I love taking her because she sees entire worlds in things I stopped looking at. There was one stall selling cheap jewelry and when we passed by she froze and her eyes became glued to an aquamarine seahorse necklace. She could not stop explaining how each of her favorite characters would love or hate it and I hope she knows how much I love her explanations.

By the time we got home and parked she was only half-way done. she said “It’ll be easier if I make a flow chart”, and raced up to her room.

I treasure the scrapbooks we’ve made from our adventures together. A million branches and she loves every one. And I worry about her, too. It’s like a poet and a warrior live in her heart and they’re still learning to share it. I caught glimpses of them seeing her tremble standing between her friends and a spider and how she cried all morning when the Spring flowers were gone.

Over lunch, I finished the shield from her XIV character that she’s been begging for for months. When I gave it to her she screamed and ran around the block with it so fast neither the dogs nor I could keep up. When she finally stopped and turned around and pushed the shield into my chest and said “if we only have one, I want you to have it. To keep you safe”.

When I asked her what will keep her safe she furrowed one brow and said “You, silly!” and that’s when I found out that that pride and grief are the same thing sometimes.

You should see how tender she is with the parts of me that I used to hide. Yesterday she tore her favorite cargo shorts climbing the tree the woodpeckers live in. She pretended she was fine but during dinner she barely ate Mom’s sinigang. So I happened to need her help afterwards to clean out the attic and when we found my old sowing machine that I haven’t used since PAX her whole face came back to life. “Is this how you made Meteion?”, and she demanded I teach her how to use it.

I watched her as she sowed and neither of us said a word for a while. I finally asked her what’s been making her sad and she said she’s not sad about the shorts. I asked her what it was really about and she paused and then got angry and said “I hate growing up”. I waited and she paused again and started tearing up, still sowing her pants.

“I miss when I was little and you could pick me up.” she said, and that made me cry and then she cried and she jumped into my arms and squeezed me tight. I don’t know for how long. But when my finally tears stopped I told her that growing up is more like realizing she will always be my little baby, and she liked that better and fell asleep on my lap.

No one told me that being their parent would be like having a compass in my heart that always points home. That no matter how much we fight and yell break each other's hearts we'll always come back together and become strong in the broken places.

I think it’s better that no one told me any of this, actually, because I got to learn it all from them.

Anyway, remember to charge the car before we head out this year. If we get stuck outside Leavenworth without AC again I told them to sing Gentleman in your ear until you promise to get us Cane’s.

Love,
